Why & How KharifShield Works — Click Any Section to Expand

Tap cards below to read full explanation

Standard weather apps only show raw rainfall numbers (like "14 mm tomorrow"), leaving the farmer to guess whether to switch on the borewell. Conversely, static agricultural pamphlets assume unlimited groundwater and recommend fixed weekly watering.

KharifShield bridges that exact gap: it combines your real borewell reserve (25%–100%), your soil's water-holding capacity (Red, Mixed, or Black), your crop's exact growth stage, and live 3-day district rainfall to give one clear, actionable decision today—protecting up to 27% more harvest than splitting water evenly across the season.

Maize does not need equal water every week. According to FAO Irrigation & Drainage Paper 33, water stress during Weeks 1–4 has a low yield penalty (Ky = 0.40), whereas stress during Weeks 5–10 (tasseling, silking, and cob formation) causes severe kernel abortion (Ky = 1.50).

Our optimizer intentionally holds back early water—encouraging deeper root growth—and reserves 55% to 65% of your scarce borewell water for Weeks 5–10, adjusting watering intervals automatically for fast-draining Red soil (5–6 days) versus moisture-retaining Black soil (9–11 days).

IoT soil probes cost ₹8,000–₹25,000 per acre, require batteries and field maintenance, and often get damaged during ploughing. Smallholder farmers in Telangana cannot afford sensor hardware.

KharifShield runs at ₹0 hardware cost in any mobile browser, works offline with district climate fallbacks when rural cellular data drops, maps local towns and suburbs (like Dammaiguda or Bachupally) to their district automatically, and reads every instruction aloud in calm, plain Telugu and English.

Every time you check your plan, KharifShield queries live 3-day district precipitation from Open-Meteo. If ≥12 mm of useful rain is expected, it pauses pumping to save a full borewell cycle.

If a heavy rainstorm or cyclone (>30 mm) approaches, an immediate red alert overrides the schedule, instructing the farmer to keep pumps off and clear field drainage channels because standing water for over 24 hours suffocates maize roots.

Methodology & Honest Scope
